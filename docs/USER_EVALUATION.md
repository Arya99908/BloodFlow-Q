# Operator evaluation protocol — not completed research

Recruit consenting blood-bank or hospital inventory professionals through the
project team. Do not collect patient records or perform real dispatch. Use only
the synthetic fixture. Obtain permission before recording or quoting feedback.

Give each participant these tasks without coaching:

1. Explain what the application allocates and what a feasible result means.
2. Run the disruption story and identify which hospital loses a critical unit.
3. Explain why the weighted policy can leave expensive demand unmet.
4. Identify what the quantum comparison proves and does not prove.
5. Export a plan and identify information missing for a real operational review.

Record completion, time, errors, prompts needed and the participant's explanation
in their own words. Ask what would prevent adoption, which constraints are
missing, and whether the shortage explanation supports a decision. Separate
observations from the team's interpretation; report unsuccessful tasks too.

Use an anonymized record per participant:

| Field | Observed value |
|---|---|
| Participant ID and relevant role | Pending |
| Consent and session date | Pending |
| Build/source hash and scenario | Pending |
| Task completion, time and assistance | Pending |
| Misunderstandings and exact feedback (with permission) | Pending |
| Proposed change and follow-up verification | Pending |

A small convenience sample can reveal usability problems. It cannot establish
clinical efficacy, market validation, improved survival or population-level impact.
Publish participant count and limitations alongside any reported findings.
