# Next research steps

Ranked by scientific value relative to cost. None presupposes a quantum advantage.

1. **Replace enumerated cost tables and feasible-state preparation.** Derive reversible arithmetic for shipment costs, unmet demand, and constraint predicates; specify ancillas, precision, uncomputation, gate counts and preparation cost. Compare against direct classical minimum-cost flow and table-free reduced simulation. Cost: moderate engineering, potentially large circuits. Change the conclusion only if total resources scale acceptably beyond exhaustive enumeration; fewer logical qubits alone is insufficient.
2. **Build and prove a residual-flow mixer.** Include slack/unmet arcs and reversible path/cycle moves that preserve each conservation law and capacity. Prove boundaries and connectivity of the feasible-state graph; exhaustively test all transitions on small cases before circuit synthesis. Cost: substantial mathematical/circuit work. A disconnected feasible space or expensive move selection rejects the construction. Evidence needed: reachable feasible optima without enumerating all feasible states and acceptable compiled resources.
3. **Test a justified hybrid neighborhood task.** Use LP-guided residual neighborhoods with identical incumbents, decomposition and budgets for quantum, MILP, greedy and classical stochastic subsolvers. Include table-building and preprocessing costs. Cost: moderate after a table-free oracle exists. Evidence needed: reproducible end-to-end improvement on a frozen larger instance set; beating an intentionally weakened greedy baseline does not qualify.
4. **Establish realistic hardness and strong classical scaling.** Obtain representative demand/stock/transport instances and identify actual requirements such as fixed dispatch costs or temporal coupling. Preserve the current flow task as a baseline; label extensions as different optimization problems. Cost: chiefly data/domain work. Evidence needed: strong LP/MILP/flow methods demonstrably challenged under a defined budget, without removing information given to quantum methods.
5. **Finite-shot and hardware-resource replication.** Only after the above gates, compare ascending CVaR and noise-tolerant optimizers using a frozen shot budget, held-out instances, and compiled circuits on a specified topology. Keep ideal controls and charge training, calibration and queue/execution costs separately. Cost: moderate simulator work, potentially expensive hardware access. Evidence needed: corrected paired comparisons and reproducible useful-solution probability/cost gains that survive sampling and gate noise. Current toy depolarizing experiments are insufficient.
