# Quantum necessity: frozen ablation protocol

2026-10-23. Written before benchmark outcomes. No change to production or prior results.

Question: does a bounded quantum neighborhood search contribute beyond its classical wrapper and preprocessing? This is an exploratory follow-up, not a confirmatory extension of the previous three-contrast family.

Six frozen scenarios (seeds 7300–7305) use 3–5 banks, 3–5 hospitals, one or two blood groups and quantities up to two. Each is tested under the original allocation objective and a hypothetical fixed dispatch fee per active bank-to-hospital route (shared across blood types), yielding twelve cases. Synthetic fees are 3–12 objective units, seeded 8300–8305. They are not calibrated costs or an asserted application requirement. No time, expiry or stochastic-demand feature is silently added.

The original objective is equivalently constant plus linear shipment coefficients. Feasibility is the original supply/demand/compatibility/route model. For dispatch extension add sum_r f_r y_r, with sum_{j on r} x_j <= M_r y_r, y_r binary and M_r=sum bounds on route. Positive f ensures an optimum does not pay for unused routes. LP relaxation may be fractional; the LP value is then a lower bound, not a valid allocation.

Methods: unchanged classical wrapper with one of (a) restricted MILP, (b) full neighborhood table minimum, (c) bounded adaptive threshold-amplitude sampling, (d) uniform random candidate sampling. Each begins at the same fee-aware deterministic greedy incumbent and receives the same pre-generated variable neighborhoods. Later incumbents may differ: that is part of the hybrid treatment. Four rounds, at most ten neighborhood bits, seeds 41–45, same 0.1-second soft cap per round. Calls to MILP and table construction are not interruptible at arbitrary Python instruction boundaries; record actual overruns and do not claim strict equal elapsed time. Classical and quantum oracle calls are not equal hardware costs.

Quantum adaptation: initial search range m=1, choose r uniformly in [0,ceil(m)), cap r at remaining budget minus one; perform r amplitude-amplification iterations and one independent measurement; accept strict improvement and reset m=1, otherwise multiply m by 1.2 up to 8. Stop at 64 total oracle iterations plus measured-candidate checks or soft wall cap. The number of marked states is not used to select iterations. This is a bounded ideal simulator implementation, not a complexity-guaranteed unrestricted GAS or hardware run. Quantum table preprocessing is charged; exact-table replacement builds and consumes the same table. Uniform control has 64 scalar candidate evaluations and is secondary to MILP/table controls, not the sole classical competitor.

Full-model MILP (2-second cap) and LP (2-second cap) provide global controls. Only certified status-0 MILP objectives are labeled optimal. Store bounds/status if limits prevent proof. Larger classical scaling uses 4, 8, 16 and 24 banks/hospitals, two groups, two seeds, original and dispatch variants; 32 rows with both LP and MILP, 2-second solver caps. These cases are not statevector simulated. No finding from a small neighborhood establishes large-scale quantum behavior.

Budgets: 240 hybrid runs and 960 bounded rounds; at most roughly 96 seconds of nominal round budgets plus setup. Larger baseline worst-case solver time roughly 128 seconds. Stage unit tests and a pilot first. Stop on correctness failure. Preserve negative outcomes.

Metrics: original/extended objectives, independently validated allocations, proven optimum gaps where known, shared initial quality, preprocessing and end-to-end simulation time, every round/attempt, table evaluations, oracle iterations, measured candidates, qubits, solver bounds/status, actual overruns. Analyze six independently generated case pairs separately for each model; five seeds are optimizer/sampler variation, not thirty independent cases. Paired differences and bootstrap intervals over case means are descriptive. No post-hoc isolated wins are promoted as advantage.

Verdict gates: (1) show a relevant classical difficulty; (2) show a useful quantum contribution against the same-wrapper classical replacement; (3) eliminate exhaustive answer-revealing preprocessing; (4) quantify real circuit/preparation cost and noise before any hardware speedup claim. Failure of any gate prevents a necessity claim. A legitimate conclusion is that quantum is not justified for the current task.
